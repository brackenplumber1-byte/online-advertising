The First 10 Minutes Matter Most

A plumbing emergency in Gauteng — a burst pipe flooding a room, a geyser letting go in the ceiling, a sewer backup — can cause thousands of rands of damage in a short time. Most of that damage doesn't happen from the initial failure. It happens in the minutes while people are in shock, not sure what to do, waiting for the plumber.

Here's a clear, step-by-step guide for the most common plumbing emergencies. Save this page. You might need it at 2am.

Burst or Leaking Pipe

Step 1: Turn off your main water supply immediately. Your main stopcock is usually at your water meter — outside the house, often near the boundary wall or inside a small chamber in the ground near the street. Turn it clockwise until it stops. This cuts water to the entire property and stops the flow.

Step 2: Open a tap inside to drain the remaining pressure in the pipes. This reduces the amount of water that continues to leak out of the burst section after the main is off.

Step 3: If water is near any electrical points — light switches, plug sockets, or your DB board — switch off the electricity at your main circuit breaker before touching anything. Water and live electricity are an extremely dangerous combination.

Step 4: Call a 24/7 plumber and take photos. Document the damage with your phone before cleaning up — your home insurer will need evidence.

Burst Geyser

Step 1: Switch off the geyser isolator switch. This is a small red switch usually on the wall near the geyser cupboard or in the ceiling void near the unit. Switching this off cuts power to the heating element and stops the situation from getting worse.

Step 2: Turn off the cold water supply to the geyser (not the main supply — the geyser has its own isolation valve on the cold water inlet pipe leading into it). Turning this off stops water continuing to fill and overflow from a damaged unit.

Step 3: Check whether water is coming through your ceiling. If it is, move furniture and valuables out from under the wet area immediately. Place buckets and towels, and take photos for your insurance claim.

Step 4: Don't try to drain the geyser yourself unless you know exactly what you're doing. A pressurised geyser contains very hot water, and an incorrect drain can cause scalding.

Blocked or Overflowing Drain

Step 1: Stop using all water in that section of the house — no flushing toilets, running taps, or using showers or basins that drain into the blocked line. Using water upstream of a blockage forces sewage to back up through the nearest lower outlet, which is often a shower or toilet.

Step 2: Locate your sewer inspection point (usually a concrete or plastic cover in the garden or driveway) and open it carefully. If sewage is visible, this confirms a main line blockage rather than an isolated drain fault.

Step 3: Don't use chemical drain cleaners on a fully blocked main line — they're ineffective on solid blockages and can damage older pipes. This needs professional hydro-jetting equipment.

No Water at All

Before calling a plumber, check two things first. One: confirm whether your neighbours also have no water — if they do, it's a council supply issue and a plumber can't fix it. Two: check whether your water account is up to date, as councils in Johannesburg, Tshwane, and Ekurhuleni can disconnect supply for arrears.

If neighbours have water but you don't, and your account is current, you likely have a burst or blocked supply pipe between the meter and the house — which is a plumber's job.

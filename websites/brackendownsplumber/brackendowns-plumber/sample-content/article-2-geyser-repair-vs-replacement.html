The Question Every Gauteng Homeowner Eventually Asks

Your geyser has stopped working — or it's leaking, making strange noises, or producing lukewarm water at best. The plumber is on his way. But before he arrives, you're already wondering: is this going to be a repair, or am I looking at a full replacement?

The honest answer depends on four things: the age of the unit, the nature of the fault, the brand and availability of parts, and what your home insurer requires. Here's how to think through each one.

When Repair Is the Right Call

Most geyser faults — especially in units under 8 years old — are repairable. The most common ones are:

  • Failed heating element — the most frequent fault, usually R500–R900 for parts and labour
  • Thermostat failure — causes overheating or no heat, typically R400–R700 to fix
  • Pressure relief valve (PRV) dripping — a safety device that needs replacement, usually R600–R1,200
  • Leaking inlet or outlet connections — a plumbing fix rather than a geyser fault, often R500–R800

If your geyser is under 8 years old and the fault is any of the above, repair is almost always the better financial decision. A replacement geyser plus installation in Gauteng typically runs between R8,000 and R18,000 depending on size and brand — so a R700 element replacement on a 5-year-old unit makes obvious sense.

When Replacement Makes More Sense

There are situations where a plumber will recommend replacement even if a repair is technically possible:

  • The unit is over 10 years old — even a successful repair is likely to be followed by another fault within 12–18 months on an older unit
  • There is visible tank corrosion — rust-coloured hot water is a sign the inner lining has failed; a repair won't fix this
  • The tank itself has cracked or is leaking at the body — this can't be repaired; replacement is the only option
  • Multiple faults in a short period — if a unit has needed two or more repairs in the past year, replacement is usually cheaper over 3 years than continued maintenance

What Your Home Insurer Needs to Know

This is the part most Gauteng homeowners don't think about until it's too late. If you're replacing a geyser and want to claim from your home insurance, your insurer will almost certainly require a valid PIRB Certificate of Compliance (CoC) — issued only by a registered plumber. An unregistered plumber cannot issue one, which means your installation won't be insurable.

The same applies to repairs on older units: if the repair involves the element or thermostat, some insurers require a CoC for the work done. It's worth asking your plumber before they start whether they're PIRB registered and whether a CoC will be issued.

What Does a Geyser Replacement Actually Cost in Gauteng?

Prices vary depending on size, brand, and whether any additional work is needed (like upgrading the drip tray or replacing the pressure valve at the same time). As a general guide for Midrand, Johannesburg, and wider Gauteng in 2025:

  • 100L electric geyser, supply and install: R8,000–R11,000
  • 150L electric geyser, supply and install: R9,500–R13,000
  • 200L electric geyser, supply and install: R11,000–R16,000
  • Solar geyser (flat plate), supply and install: R18,000–R28,000
  • Heat pump water heater, supply and install: R22,000–R35,000

These figures include the unit, labour, a new drip tray if required, and the PIRB Certificate of Compliance. They don't include any ceiling repairs if water damage occurred from a burst unit.

The Practical Answer

If you're unsure, the right move is to have a PIRB-registered plumber inspect the unit before making a decision. A proper inspection takes 10–15 minutes and will tell you the age, condition, and likely remaining life of the unit — giving you the information to make the right call rather than guessing.

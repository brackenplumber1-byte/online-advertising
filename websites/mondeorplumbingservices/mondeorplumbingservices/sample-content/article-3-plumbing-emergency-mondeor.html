What You Do in the First Few Minutes Matters More Than You'd Think

A burst pipe, a geyser letting go in the ceiling, or a sewer line backing up can do a surprising amount of damage fast — and most of it happens not from the failure itself, but from the minutes people spend unsure what to do while waiting for help. This is a practical, step-by-step guide for the plumbing emergencies we see most often across Mondeor and the rest of Johannesburg South. Worth bookmarking — you're more likely to need it at an inconvenient hour than a convenient one.

Burst or Leaking Pipe

1. Shut off your main water supply. Your main stopcock is usually near your water meter — outside the house, often close to the boundary wall or the street. Turn it clockwise until it stops. This cuts water to the whole property immediately.

2. Open an indoor tap to relieve the pressure still in the pipes. This limits how much water keeps escaping from the burst section even after the main is shut.

3. If water is anywhere near electrics — plug points, light switches, your DB board — switch off the mains circuit breaker before you touch anything else. Don't take chances with water and live electricity.

4. Call us and take photos before you start cleaning up. Your insurer will want evidence of the damage, and photos taken before cleanup are worth far more than a description after the fact.

Burst or Leaking Geyser

1. Switch off the geyser's isolator switch — a small red switch usually near the geyser cupboard or in the ceiling void close to the unit. This cuts power to the element immediately.

2. Close the geyser's own cold water inlet valve — not the main supply to the house, the geyser's dedicated isolation valve on the pipe feeding into it. This stops the unit continuing to fill and overflow.

3. Check for water coming through the ceiling. If you see damp patches or dripping, move furniture and anything valuable out from underneath straight away, get buckets and towels down, and photograph it for insurance.

4. Don't attempt to drain the geyser yourself. A pressurised geyser holds very hot water, and draining it incorrectly can scald. Leave that part to us.

Blocked or Overflowing Drain

1. Stop using water anywhere upstream of the blockage — no flushing, no running taps or showers feeding into that line. Water forced through a blocked pipe tends to back up out of the nearest lower fixture, which is often a shower or toilet.

2. If you can locate your sewer inspection point (usually a cover in the garden or driveway), open it carefully. Sewage visible there points to a main line blockage rather than a smaller, isolated one.

3. Skip the chemical drain cleaner. On a proper main-line blockage they rarely work, and on older Johannesburg South pipework they can do more harm than good. This is a job for hydro-jetting equipment, not a bottle from the hardware store.

No Water at All

Before calling us, check two quick things. First: do your neighbours have water? If not, it's a City of Johannesburg supply issue, not something a plumber can fix — check the City Power/Johannesburg Water outage reports. Second: is your municipal account current? The City of Johannesburg can and does disconnect supply for arrears.

If your neighbours have water and your account's in order, the fault is most likely a burst or blocked pipe between your meter and the house — and that part is exactly what we handle.

The Question That Comes Up on Almost Every Call-Out

Your geyser has stopped working, is leaking, or is making a noise it's never made before. Before we even arrive, most Mondeor homeowners are already asking the same thing: is this a repair, or do I need a whole new unit?

There isn't a single answer that applies to every geyser — but there is a short list of things that actually decide it. Here's how we work through it on-site, so you know what to expect before we get there.

Age Is the First Thing We Check

Most geysers in Johannesburg South homes are electric, and most electric geysers have a working life of around 8 to 12 years, depending on water quality and how hard the unit has worked. If your geyser is under 8 years old, repair is almost always the sensible option regardless of what's gone wrong — the unit still has years of useful life ahead of it. If it's past 10-12 years and something has failed, we'll usually walk you through why replacement tends to make more financial sense, rather than just recommending it outright.

Common Faults That Are Almost Always Worth Repairing

  • Failed heating element — the single most common geyser fault; a straightforward, relatively inexpensive repair
  • Faulty thermostat — causes no heat or overheating; usually a same-visit fix
  • Dripping pressure relief valve — a safety component that wears out and simply gets replaced
  • Leaking inlet or outlet fittings — often a plumbing connection issue rather than a fault with the tank itself

None of these mean the geyser itself is failing — they're wearable parts, and replacing them on a unit that's otherwise in good condition is the right call in the large majority of cases we see.

Signs That Point to Replacement Instead

  • Rust-coloured water — a sign the inner tank lining has started to fail; this can't be repaired, only delayed
  • A crack or leak in the tank body itself — as opposed to a fitting or valve, this means the tank is done
  • Repeated faults within 12 months — if we've been out twice already this year, a third repair is rarely the better financial decision over a new unit
  • Unit older than 10-12 years with any significant fault — even a successful repair on a unit this age tends to buy limited extra time

The Insurance Question Most People Don't Think of Until It Matters

If you ever need to claim on your home insurance for water damage from a geyser failure, your insurer will almost certainly want a valid PIRB Certificate of Compliance for the work done — whether that's a repair involving the element or thermostat, or a full replacement. An unregistered plumber can't issue one, which quietly makes the work uninsurable even if it was done correctly. Always confirm PIRB registration before work starts, not after.

What It Typically Costs

The figures below are general, indicative ranges — actual pricing depends on your specific unit, access, and any additional work needed. Always get an exact quote before agreeing to any job.

  • Element or thermostat replacement: a fraction of the cost of a new geyser — usually the first thing worth trying on units under 8 years old
  • Pressure relief valve replacement: a small, quick job in most cases
  • Full geyser replacement (100L-200L electric, supply and install): a significantly larger cost than any single repair, but often the right call once a unit is past 10-12 years or the tank itself has failed
  • Solar or heat pump systems: a higher upfront cost than standard electric replacement, offset over time by lower running costs — worth asking about if you're replacing anyway
